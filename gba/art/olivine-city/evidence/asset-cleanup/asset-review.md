## Receiving surfaces and cliff tiles

Grass, paving, sand, deck and water cores were reviewed individually and as 4×4 repetitions. They retain their material patterns. Ground beneath every replaced foreground outline was restored independently; the sea-rock at the terminal platform needed pixel-level water restoration because its metatile also contains deck.

All 426 distinct stone authoring glyphs were checked by the constrained cleanup pass; 112 isolated low-contrast stone pixels were removed. Full interior stone uses same-facing, edge-weighted source medoids to reserve native space for complete architecture. Mixed-material edges, caps, silhouettes, corners and wall masks remain intact. Full map regions and the eight native tile sheets were inspected visually after compilation.

The 19 foreground families have 320 assembly records, representing 319 distinct placements (the lighthouse has a retained duplicate stamp). Every visible placed master pixel is checked against actual decoded native output. The 1,289 used metatiles cover all 4,032 native cells. Pixel agreement is an integration check, not proof of artistic quality or owner acceptance.

## Limits

Original hidden terrain and collision are overview interpretations. Native building footprints and simplified decorative treatment differ from HGSS; this pass cleans existing placed elements and preserves the established layout, rather than claiming a pixel-identical original scene. Water remains static. Interiors, Route 39/40 connections and shipping services are unwired. District transitions use fades. Quay has only three spare native glyphs, so future additions need deliberate budgeting. Visual acceptance remains with the owner.
